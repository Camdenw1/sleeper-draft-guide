Projections, four public boards, and where the money went.

Blended from a projection model rescored for your exact scoring, four public ranking systems, and the betting market.

What the rank is

Half projection model, half public consensus, nudged by the betting market. The model takes season projections and rescores them for this league: half PPR, 0.75 per catch for tight ends (0.5 baseline plus your 0.25 premium), and yardage bonuses of +3 at 100 yards and +2 more at 200 (+3 at 300 and +2 at 400 passing). Those points are measured against replacement level for your 12-team roster — QB, 2RB, 2WR, TE, RB/WR/TE flex, WR/TE flex, K, DST — so value is scarcity-adjusted rather than raw points.

Why it isn't pure model

Every projection comes from a single source. Where that source is stingy, a pure model rank fought all four public boards on one house's opinion. So each player's average public rank is converted back into points using the model's own value curve, and the two are averaged. The Model column shows the unblended view; the distance between it and the rank is how far the crowd pulled him.

The four systems

All four are free public sources, pulled live at build time. FFC is Fantasy Football Calculator's real half-PPR 12-team ADP from thousands of public mock drafts — the only source whose format matches this league exactly, which is why it anchors the blend. ESPN and Yahoo are their public draft boards; Sleeper is its draft-room ordering. Every system gives every player exactly one slot, so the spread plot is directly comparable across all four.

Market column

Free season-long props existed for only about 30 players. Where one exists, the score compares the book's line to the projection behind that player's rank. For everyone else it falls back to team win total, which is a team-strength proxy rather than an offence-specific one — so it carries about half the weight. Hover any market number to see the underlying line.

Tiers

Bands are drops in value, not even buckets. Viewing everyone shows overall tiers; filtering to a position switches the bands to that position's own tiers, which is what matters when you're deciding whether to take the last back in a tier or wait a round.

Known soft spots

The model doesn't simulate role changes, so a backup is valued at his projected volume rather than what he'd get if the starter went down — spend late picks on handcuffs regardless of what the board says. Deep bench players are smoothed across 17 games. And the board inherits its projection source's opinions; where you disagree, override it.
