Projections, three ADP boards, and where the money went.

Blended from a projection model rescored for your exact scoring, three public ADP sources, and the betting market.

What the rank is

Half projection model, half public consensus, nudged by the betting market. The model takes season projections and rescores them for this league: half PPR, 0.75 per catch for tight ends (0.5 baseline plus your 0.25 premium), interceptions at −2, and yardage bonuses of +3 for a 100–199 yard game and +4 for 200+ (+3 at 300–399 and +4 at 400+ passing). Those tiers are exclusive: a 210-yard game pays 4, not 7. Kickers use normal scoring (3 under 40, 4 from 40–49, 5 from 50+). Those points are measured against replacement level for your 12-team roster — QB, 2RB, 2WR, TE, RB/WR/TE flex, WR/TE flex, K, DST — so value is scarcity-adjusted rather than raw points.

Why it isn't pure model

Every projection comes from a single source. Where that source is stingy, a pure model rank fought all three public boards on one house's opinion. So each player's average public rank is converted back into points using the model's own value curve, and the two are averaged. The Model column shows the unblended view; the distance between it and the rank is how far the crowd pulled him.

The three systems

All three are free public sources, pulled live at build time, and all three are real average draft position — what people actually did, not an editor's list. FFC is Fantasy Football Calculator's half-PPR 12-team ADP from thousands of public mock drafts, the only source whose format matches this league exactly, which is why it anchors the blend. Every system gives every player exactly one slot, so the spread plot is directly comparable across all four.

Market column

Free season-long props existed for only about 30 players. Where one exists, the score compares the book's line to the projection behind that player's rank. For everyone else it falls back to team win total, which is a team-strength proxy rather than an offence-specific one — so it carries about half the weight. Hover any market number to see the underlying line.

Tiers

Bands are drops in value, not even buckets. Viewing everyone shows overall tiers; filtering to a position switches the bands to that position's own tiers, which is what matters when you're deciding whether to take the last back in a tier or wait a round.

Known soft spots

The model doesn't simulate role changes, so a backup is valued at his projected volume rather than what he'd get if the starter went down — spend late picks on handcuffs regardless of what the board says. Deep bench players are smoothed across 17 games. And the board inherits its projection source's opinions; where you disagree, override it.
